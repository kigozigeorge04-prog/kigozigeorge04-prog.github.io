💡 Abdominal Pain
  • Site, onset, character, radiation, severity, timing, exacerbating/relieving, associated features.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Sudden onset, rigidity, obstruction, instability.
💡 Abdominal Swelling
  • Location, onset, size change, associated weight loss, hernia screen, pulsatility.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Rapid growth, irreducible hernia, AAA.
💡 Nausea / Vomiting
  • Content, timing, projectile, volume, dehydration.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Haematemesis, faeculent, dehydration.
💡 Bowel Change
  • Nature, stool character, absolute constipation, blood, tenesmus.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Absolute constipation, PR bleed + weight loss.
💡 Rectal Bleeding
  • Colour, relation to stool, volume, pain, mucus.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Melaena, large volume, weight loss.
💡 Jaundice
  • Type, dark urine/pale stool, pain, fever, alcohol risk.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Charcot's triad, Reynolds' pentad, painless progressive.
💡 Haematuria
  • Timing, clots, pain, associated LUTS, schistosomiasis.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Painless total, flank mass, clot retention.
💡 Urinary Symptoms
  • Obstructive/irritative, retention, discharge.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Acute retention, haematuria + weight loss, neurological.
💡 Scrotal
  • Age context, onset, tenderness, transillumination.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Sudden pain in adolescent, hard mass, irreducible hernia.
💡 Surface Swelling
  • Site, depth, growth, consistency, tenderness, skin changes.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Rapid growth, matted nodes, pulsatile.
💡 Wound / Ulcer
  • Duration, edge, base, discharge, pain, diabetes, tropical causes.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Necrotising fasciitis, Marjolin's ulcer, joint involvement.
💡 Trauma
  • Mechanism, time, LOC, regions, bleeding, neurovascular, tetanus.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Instability, tension pneumothorax, spinal injury, open fracture.
💡 Bone/Joint Pain
  • Site, onset, swelling, fever, function, TB, sickle.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Septic joint, bone pain + weight loss, cauda equina.
💡 Fracture
  • Mechanism, open/closed, deformity, neurovascular, compartment.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Open, absent pulse, compartment syndrome, spinal.
💡 Breast
  • Lump, discharge, pain, risk factors, axillary nodes.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Hard irregular lump, bloody discharge, peau d'orange.
💡 Neck/Thyroid
  • Location, swallowing, thyroid status, compressive symptoms, TB.
🚨 Red Flags
  • Hoarseness, stridor, hard fixed mass.
💡 General
  • SOCRATES, lump characteristics, systemic features, function.
💡 Loin Pain
  • Site: Unilateral/bilateral, flank, radiating to groin/genitalia
  • Onset: Sudden/gradual, relation to movement
  • Character: Sharp/dull/colicky (renal colic is intermittent, severe)
  • Severity: 0-10 scale, associated nausea/vomiting
  • Urinary: Dysuria, frequency, haematuria, fever
  • Systemic: Fever, chills, malaise
  • Past: History of stones, UTIs, schistosomiasis (common in Uganda)
🚨 Red Flags
  • Anuria/oliguria — acute kidney injury
  • High fever with rigors — pyelonephritis/sepsis
  • Painless haematuria in older patient — renal malignancy
  • Bilateral loin pain with anuria — urinary obstruction
💡 Prostate Symptoms
  • Obstructive: Poor stream, hesitancy, terminal dribbling, incomplete emptying
  • Irritative: Frequency, urgency, nocturia, dysuria
  • Duration: Gradual onset over months/years vs acute
  • Acute retention: Complete inability to void, severe suprapubic pain
  • Systemic: Weight loss, bone pain (metastases), fever (prostatitis)
  • Risk factors: Age >50, family history, African descent
🚨 Red Flags
  • Acute urinary retention — emergency
  • Bone pain + raised PSA — metastatic prostate cancer
  • Haematuria with obstructive symptoms — bladder/prostate malignancy
  • Uraemic symptoms (nausea, confusion, pruritus)
💡 Back Pain
  • Site: Cervical/thoracic/lumbar/sacral, midline/paraspinal
  • Onset: Acute (lifting/trauma) vs insidious
  • Character: Aching, stabbing, burning, radicular
  • Radiation: To buttock, leg (sciatica), groin
  • Exacerbating: Movement, coughing, sneezing
  • Relieving: Rest, lying still, specific positions
  • Neurological: Weakness, numbness, bladder/bowel dysfunction
🚨 Red Flags
  • Cauda equina syndrome — bilateral leg weakness, saddle anaesthesia, urinary retention
  • Vertebral fracture — after trauma, in elderly/osteoporosis
  • Spinal infection — fever, night pain, immunocompromised
  • Malignancy — unrelenting pain, night pain, weight loss
💡 Joint Swelling
  • Joint(s): Monoarticular vs polyarticular, small vs large joints
  • Onset: Acute (hours-days) vs chronic (weeks-months)
  • Character: Hot, swollen, red, tender (inflammatory) vs mechanical
  • Pain: At rest, with movement, night pain
  • Stiffness: Morning stiffness duration (inflammatory >60 mins)
  • Systemic: Fever, rash, weight loss, eye symptoms
  • Risk factors: Trauma, infection, sickle cell (Uganda), gout, autoimmune
🚨 Red Flags
  • Septic arthritis — hot, swollen, systemically unwell, emergency
  • Haemarthrosis in sickle cell — painful crisis
  • Rapid joint destruction — inflammatory arthropathy
  • Joint with overlying skin changes — TB, fungal
💡 Skin Lesion
  • Appearance: Nodule, plaque, ulcer, blister, scaling, pigmented
  • Site: Sun-exposed vs covered, trauma-prone areas
  • Duration: Since when, how fast growing (weeks, months, years)
  • Change: Size, shape, colour, bleeding, itching
  • Risk factors: Sun exposure, HIV, TB, diabetes, tropical infections
  • Regional nodes: Swollen, hard, tender
🚨 Red Flags
  • Rapid growth — malignancy (SCC, melanoma, Kaposi)
  • Ulcer with raised everted edges — Marjolin's ulcer (SCC in scar)
  • Bleeding, crusting, non-healing — suspicious
  • Multiple lesions + weight loss — systemic malignancy
💡 Lymph Node Swelling
  • Site: Cervical, axillary, inguinal, supraclavicular (Virchow's node)
  • Size: Measure in cm, number of nodes
  • Consistency: Soft, firm, rubbery, hard (malignancy)
  • Mobility: Mobile vs fixed to underlying structures
  • Tenderness: Tender (infection) vs painless (malignancy)
  • Systemic: Fever, weight loss, night sweats, fatigue
  • Associated: Sore throat, skin infection, trauma
🚨 Red Flags
  • Supraclavicular nodes — visceral malignancy (Virchow's sign)
  • Hard, fixed, painless nodes — lymphoma or metastasis
  • Generalised lymphadenopathy + B symptoms — lymphoma
  • Tropical infections — TB, HIV, brucellosis
💡 Breast Pain
  • Site: Unilateral/bilateral, focal/diffuse
  • Character: Aching, burning, stabbing, cyclical
  • Relation to menstrual cycle: Premenstrual vs constant
  • Associated: Lump, nipple discharge, skin changes, fever
  • Risk factors: Age, family history, HRT, pregnancy
  • Trauma: History of injury to breast
🚨 Red Flags
  • Focal persistent pain in postmenopausal woman — malignancy
  • Pain + lump + skin dimpling — inflammatory breast cancer
  • Pain + fever + redness — mastitis/abscess (requires antibiotics/aspiration)
💡 Nipple Discharge
  • Nature: Milky, serous, bloody, purulent, green
  • Unilateral/bilateral: Unilateral more concerning
  • Single/multiple ducts: Single duct pathology
  • Spontaneous or provoked: Spontaneous more concerning
  • Associated: Lump, pain, skin changes
  • Risk factors: Prolactinoma, medications, pregnancy
🚨 Red Flags
  • Bloody discharge — intraductal papilloma, carcinoma
  • Unilateral spontaneous discharge in older woman
  • Persistent discharge + lump
💡 Varicose Veins
  • Onset: When first noticed, progression over time
  • Location: Great saphenous, small saphenous, perforators
  • Symmetry: Unilateral (consider DVT) vs bilateral
  • Pain: Aching, heaviness, worsened by standing
  • Complications: Bleeding, ulceration, thrombophlebitis, skin changes
  • Risk factors: Pregnancy, obesity, prolonged standing, family history
🚨 Red Flags
  • Sudden painful swollen leg — DVT
  • Ulceration over veins — venous ulcer
  • Bleeding from varicose vein — emergency
  • Skin changes (lipodermatosclerosis) — chronic venous insufficiency
💡 Leg Ulcer
  • Location: Venous (gaiter area) vs arterial (toes, heels)
  • Appearance: Edge, base, discharge, surrounding skin
  • Duration: How long, changes over time
  • Pain: Painful (arterial) vs relatively painless (venous/neuropathic)
  • Associated: Varicose veins, diabetes, smoking, hypertension
  • Vascular: Pulses, sensation, capillary refill
🚨 Red Flags
  • Arterial ulcer with absent pulses — critical limb ischaemia
  • Diabetic foot ulcer — risk of osteomyelitis, amputation
  • Rapidly progressing ulcer — necrotising fasciitis
  • Non-healing ulcer >6 weeks — consider malignancy
💡 Claudication
  • Location: Calf, thigh, buttock (aortoiliac disease)
  • Distance: How far can walk before symptoms
  • Relief: Relieved by rest, time to resolve
  • Rest pain: Pain at night, relieved by hanging leg down
  • Skin changes: Hair loss, shiny skin, colour change
  • Risk factors: Smoking, diabetes, hypertension, hyperlipidaemia
🚨 Red Flags
  • Rest pain — critical ischaemia (Rutherford category 4)
  • Rapid deterioration — acute limb ischaemia (emergency)
  • Gangrene — tissue necrosis
  • Absent pulses + pallor + cold limb
💡 Hernia
  • Site: Inguinal (direct/indirect), femoral, umbilical, incisional, epigastric
  • Onset: Gradual vs sudden after straining
  • Reducibility: Reducible, irreducible, obstructed
  • Pain: Discomfort, dragging, sudden severe (strangulation)
  • Associated: Vomiting, absolute constipation (obstruction)
  • Risk factors: Chronic cough, constipation, heavy lifting, pregnancy
🚨 Red Flags
  • Irreducible + tender + signs of obstruction — incarcerated hernia
  • Tender, tense, non-reducible hernia with systemic signs — strangulation (emergency)
  • Absolute constipation + vomiting — bowel obstruction
  • Reduced femoral hernia in women — high risk of strangulation
💡 Haemorrhoids
  • Grade: I (bleed only), II (prolapse, reduce spontaneously), III (reduce manually), IV (irreducible)
  • Symptoms: Bright red bleeding, prolapse, pain (thrombosed), itching, mucus discharge
  • Duration: Acute vs chronic, recurrent
  • Precipitating: Constipation, straining, pregnancy, chronic diarrhoea
  • Other: Always consider rectal cancer in >50 with PR bleeding
🚨 Red Flags
  • PR bleeding in >50 — investigate for colorectal cancer
  • Painful acutely — thrombosed haemorrhoid
  • Bleeding + weight loss + change in bowel habit — malignancy
  • Acute painful lump — thrombosed external pile
💡 Abscess
  • Site: Skin/subcutaneous, deep, intra-abdominal, perianal
  • Onset: Days to weeks, preceding infection/injury
  • Character: Tender, warm, fluctuant, erythematous
  • Systemic: Fever, chills, malaise, anorexia
  • Risk factors: Diabetes, HIV, immunocompromise, foreign body
🚨 Red Flags
  • Signs of sepsis — fever, tachycardia, hypotension
  • Rapidly expanding abscess — necrotising soft tissue infection
  • Deep abscess — requires imaging for diagnosis
  • Perianal abscess in diabetic — high risk of progression

8 Physical Examination
Collapsible sections: Click the header to expand/collapse. Each system is broken down by Inspection, Palpation, Percussion, and Auscultation where appropriate. Hints are shown outside the text boxes so you can always refer to them while typing.
🩺 General Appearance & Vitals
▼
Look for: Well/unwell, distressed, pale, jaundiced, cachectic, alert, confused, toxic appearance, dehydration, respiratory distress.
❤️ Cardiovascular Examination
▼
🔍 Inspection
Look for: Jugular venous pressure (JVP), visible pulsations, scars, chest wall deformity, peripheral oedema, cyanosis, clubbing.
🖐 Palpation
Feel for: Apex beat (position, character), heaves, thrills, peripheral pulses (radial, brachial, femoral, dorsalis pedis) – rate, rhythm, volume, symmetry. Capillary refill time.
👂 Auscultation
Listen for: Heart sounds (S1, S2), added sounds (S3, S4), murmurs (timing, location, radiation, intensity), bruits over carotids, femorals, renal arteries.
🫁 Respiratory Examination
▼
🔍 Inspection
Look for: Respiratory rate, pattern, use of accessory muscles, tracheal position, chest symmetry, scars, deformities (kyphosis, scoliosis), colour of skin.
🖐 Palpation
Feel for: Tracheal deviation, chest expansion (symmetry), tactile fremitus/vocal fremitus, tenderness, lumps.
👂 Percussion
Listen for: Percussion note – resonant, hyperresonant, dull, stony dull. Compare both sides.
👂 Auscultation
Listen for: Breath sounds (vesicular, bronchial), added sounds – crackles, wheeze, rhonchi, pleural rub. Vocal resonance.
🫃 Abdominal Examination
▼
🔍 Inspection
Look for: Shape, symmetry, scars, striae, veins (caput medusae), visible peristalsis, hernias, distension, masses.
👂 Auscultation
Listen for: Bowel sounds (normal, absent, hyperactive, tinkling), bruits (aortic, renal, iliac).
🖐 Palpation
Feel for: Superficial (tenderness, guarding, rigidity), deep (masses, organomegaly – liver, spleen, kidneys), hernia orifices, cough impulse.
👂 Percussion
Listen for: Dullness (liver span, splenic dullness, ascites – shifting dullness), tympany.
💧 Genitourinary Examination
▼
🔍 Inspection
Look for: External genitalia – penis, urethral meatus, scrotum, testicles, skin changes, swelling, discharge.
🖐 Palpation
Feel for: Testicular size, consistency, tenderness, masses, epididymis, spermatic cord, hernias.
👂 Percussion
Listen for: Suprapubic dullness (bladder distension).
🖐 Digital Rectal Examination (DRE)
Assess: Sphincter tone, prostate (males) – size, consistency, nodules, tenderness; rectal masses, blood on finger (glove), stool colour/consistency. In females – assess posterior vaginal wall, cervix, uterosacral ligaments.
🧠 Neurological Examination
▼
🔍 Inspection & Mental Status
Assess: GCS (E/V/M), pupil size & reaction, speech, level of consciousness, posture.
🖐 Motor & Sensory
Test: Power (MRC grade), tone (spastic/rigid/flaccid), coordination (finger-nose, heel-shin), sensation (light touch, pain, proprioception), reflexes (biceps, triceps, knee, ankle, plantar response).
🖐 Cranial Nerves
Test: Olfactory to hypoglossal – as clinically indicated.
🦴 Musculoskeletal Examination
▼
🔍 Inspection
Look for: Deformity, swelling, discolouration, muscle wasting, limb length discrepancy, skin changes (ulcers, scars).
🖐 Palpation
Feel for: Tenderness, warmth, crepitus, joint effusion, bone alignment, peripheral pulses, capillary refill, sensation distal to injury.
🔄 Range of Motion
Assess: Active and passive ROM; pain, restriction, instability.
🩹 Wound / Ulcer Examination
▼
🔍 Inspection
Look for: Site, size (measure in cm), number of wounds. Edge: sloping (healing), punched-out (trophic), undermined (TB), everted (carcinoma), rolled (BCC). Base: slough, granulation, pale/ischaemic, exposed bone/tendon. Discharge: serous, purulent, haemorrhagic, watery. Surrounding skin: erythema, warmth, induration, cellulitis.
🖐 Palpation
Feel for: Tenderness, temperature, subcutaneous emphysema (crepitus), deep extension, sinus tracts, regional lymphadenopathy. Assess for distal pulses and sensation if on a limb.
👂 Auscultation / Special
Listen for: Bruits over a vascular ulcer. Probe for sinus tracts using a sterile probe (if appropriate).
➕ Other Systems (Skin, Breast, Neck, Lymphatics)
▼
🔍 Inspection & Palpation
Look and feel for: Skin: rashes, pigmentation, ulcers, surgical scars, pressure areas. Lymph nodes: cervical, axillary, inguinal – size, consistency, tenderness, mobility. Breast: inspection (symmetry, peau d'orange, nipple retraction), palpation (quadrants, axillary tail). Thyroid: inspection, palpation (size, consistency, nodules, tracheal deviation), auscultation for bruit.
