🌡️ Fever — Clinical Guide
📋 Key Questions
  • Duration: How long has the fever been present?
  • Pattern: Is it continuous, intermittent, or remittent?
  • Highest temperature: What is the maximum temperature recorded?
  • Rigors: Does the child have shaking chills?
  • Associated symptoms: Any cough, runny nose, vomiting, diarrhoea, rash?
  • Feeding: Is the child eating and drinking normally?
  • Activity: Is the child playful or lethargic?
  • Convulsions: Has the child had any fits during the fever?
  • Medications: Has the child received any antipyretics or antibiotics?
  • Exposures: Any recent travel, sick contacts, or mosquito exposure?
🤧 Cough — Clinical Guide
📋 Key Questions
  • Duration: How long has the cough been present?
  • Productive: Is it dry or productive? If productive, what colour is the sputum?
  • Timing: Is it worse at night, morning, or after meals?
  • Breathing: Is there any difficulty breathing, wheeze, or stridor?
  • Associated fever: Is there any fever?
  • Feeding: Is the child feeding normally?
  • Weight loss: Has there been any weight loss or night sweats?
  • TB contact: Has the child been in contact with anyone with chronic cough or TB?
  • Past chest infections: Has the child had asthma or recurrent pneumonia?
  • Treatment: Has the child received any antibiotics or bronchodilators?
💩 Diarrhoea / Vomiting — Clinical Guide
📋 Key Questions
  • Duration: How long has diarrhoea been present?
  • Frequency: How many stools per day?
  • Character: Is it watery, bloody, mucoid, or rice‑water?
  • Vomiting: Is there vomiting? If yes, is it bilious or projectile?
  • Fever: Is there associated fever?
  • Abdominal pain: Is there any pain or distension?
  • Dehydration signs: Is the child drinking well? Any sunken eyes, reduced skin turgor, lethargy?
  • Blood in stool: Is there any blood or mucus?
  • Recent antibiotic use: Has the child had any antibiotics recently?
  • Exposures: Any sick contacts or unsafe water?
🍼 Poor Feeding — Clinical Guide
📋 Key Questions
  • Duration: How long has poor feeding been present?
  • Description: Is the child refusing feeds, weak sucking, or tiring easily?
  • Vomiting: Is there vomiting after feeds? If yes, character?
  • Fever: Is there any fever?
  • Lethargy: Is the child lethargic or irritable?
  • Jaundice: Is there any yellow discoloration?
  • Convulsions: Has the child had any fits?
  • Weight gain: Is the child gaining weight?
  • Breathing: Is there any difficulty breathing?
  • Oral thrush: Are there white patches in the mouth?
⚠️ Convulsions — Clinical Guide
📋 Key Questions
  • Duration: How long did the seizure last?
  • Type: Was it tonic‑clonic, focal, or absence?
  • Generalised/focal: Was it generalised or focal?
  • Fever: Did the child have fever during the seizure?
  • Previous seizures: Has this happened before?
  • Post‑ictal state: Is the child back to normal, drowsy, or comatose?
  • Vomiting/headache: Any vomiting or headache before the seizure?
  • Neck stiffness: Any neck stiffness?
  • Trauma: Has there been any head injury?
  • Family history: Any family history of epilepsy?
🔴 Skin Rash — Clinical Guide
📋 Key Questions
  • Onset: When did the rash first appear?
  • Appearance: What does it look like (spots, vesicles, wheals, petechiae)?
  • Distribution: Where did it start and where is it now?
  • Itching: Is it itchy?
  • Fever: Is there associated fever?
  • Mouth lesions: Are there any blisters or ulcers in the mouth?
  • Blanching: Does the rash blanch with pressure?
  • Medications: Has the child taken any medications recently?
  • Contact: Any contact with infected individuals or allergens?
  • Joint pain: Is there any joint pain or swelling?
🟡 Jaundice — Clinical Guide
📋 Key Questions
  • Onset: When was jaundice first noticed?
  • Age: What is the child's age (neonate vs older)?
  • Feeding: Is the child feeding well?
  • Lethargy: Is the child lethargic?
  • Stool colour: What colour are the stools (normal, pale, dark)?
  • Urine colour: Is the urine dark?
  • Fever: Is there fever?
  • Vomiting: Is there vomiting?
  • Weight gain: Is the child gaining weight?
  • Family history: Any family history of jaundice or anaemia?
🩺 Pain — SOCRATES
📋 SOCRATES Framework
  • Site: Where is the pain? (point with one finger)
  • Onset: When did it start? Sudden or gradual?
  • Character: Is it sharp, dull, colicky, burning?
  • Radiation: Does it spread anywhere?
  • Associations: Any nausea, vomiting, fever, diarrhoea?
  • Timing: Constant or intermittent?
  • Exacerbating: What makes it worse?
  • Relieving: What makes it better?
  • Severity: On a scale of 0–10, how severe?
  • Previous episodes: Has this happened before?
💨 Difficulty Breathing — Clinical Guide
📋 Key Questions
  • Onset: When did the breathing difficulty start? Sudden or gradual?
  • Work of breathing: Is the child working hard to breathe (indrawing, flaring, grunting)?
  • Sounds: Is there wheeze, stridor, or grunting?
  • Cough: Is there a cough?
  • Fever: Is there fever?
  • Feeding: Is the child able to feed?
  • Speech: Can the child talk/speak?
  • Cyanosis: Is there any blue colour around the mouth or nails?
  • Choking: Has the child choked on anything?
  • Past history: Any history of asthma or allergies?
💧 Swelling — Clinical Guide
📋 Key Questions
  • Location: Where is the swelling (face, neck, abdomen, limbs, scrotum)?
  • Duration: How long has it been present?
  • Onset: Sudden or gradual?
  • Pain: Is it painful or painless?
  • Consistency: Is it hard, soft, or fluctuant?
  • Fever: Is there fever?
  • Weight loss: Has there been weight loss?
  • Urinary symptoms: Any difficulty passing urine?
  • Trauma: Any history of injury?
  • Reducibility: Can it be pushed back (hernia)?

💡 General Clinical Prompts
📋 Always Remember
  • Always check danger signs first (lethargy, inability to feed, convulsions).
  • For pain: use SOCRATES framework.
  • In Uganda: always screen for malaria and HIV.
  • Red flags are medical emergencies — do not delay referral.
  • Document fully to guide differential diagnosis.
